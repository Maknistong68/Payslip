# Saudi Arabia: Legal and Compliance Requirements for Issuing and Electronically Delivering Payslips (كشف/قسيمة الراتب)

Scope: KSA only, state as of October 2026. Primary texts used: Labour Law (نظام العمل, Royal Decree M/51 of 23/8/1426H, as amended by M/46 of 1436H/2015 and M/44 of 8/2/1446H/2024), Implementing Regulations of the Labour Law (اللائحة التنفيذية لنظام العمل, Ministerial Decision 115921 of 19/8/1446H, in force 20/8/1446H = 19 Feb 2025), MHRSD Wage Protection Program (برنامج حماية الأجور) rules, the Personal Data Protection Law (نظام حماية البيانات الشخصية, Royal Decree M/19 of 2021 as amended by M/148 of 2023) and its Implementing and Transfer Regulations, and the Evidence Law (نظام الإثبات, 1443H). Arabic terms are given where useful. Translations of the Labour Law are "for guidance; the Arabic text governs" per the official translator's note.

Headline conclusion for the report writer: **There is no article in the Saudi Labour Law or its Implementing Regulations that expressly obliges an employer to hand each worker a periodic payslip, nor one that prescribes payslip contents, a worker signature, or a payslip-specific retention period.** The obligations that functionally substitute for a payslip regime are (a) the employer's duty under Labour Law Art. 17 + IR Art. 5 to keep a wage register (كشف أجور العمال) showing each wage, its receipt date, every deduction and its reason, in paper or electronic form; (b) mandatory bank payment under Art. 90(2); and (c) the Wage Protection Program (Mudad) wage file, which MHRSD describes as the reference for wage disputes and as the mechanism that lets employers "prove wage payment without receipt sheets". Electronic delivery is therefore lawful; the live constraints are evidentiary (Evidence Law digital-evidence rules) and PDPL (security, lawful basis, cross-border transfer when using foreign-hosted consumer apps).

## Key Question 1: Which article(s) of the Labour Law / Implementing Regulations require a written or electronic statement of wages and deductions, and what must it contain?

### Takeaway
No Labour Law or Implementing Regulation article requires a per-worker payslip. The statutory anchor is Labour Law Art. 17 (employer must keep at the workplace the records, statements and files specified in the Regulations) implemented by IR Art. 5, which mandates a "wage statement/register of workers" (كشف أجور العمال) containing wages, dates the worker received them, amounts deducted and the reason for each deduction, expressly "whether paper or electronic". Vendor claims that "Article 70" mandates payslips, or that 2025 amendments require monthly e-payslips with 5-year retention, are not supported by the primary text.

### Cited Findings
- Labour Law Art. 17: "An employer shall maintain, at the workplace, records, statements, and files the nature and contents of which shall be specified in the Regulations. He shall display at a visible location at the workplace a schedule of working hours, breaks, weekly rest days..." — [Labour Law, HRDF English consolidation (Royal Decree M/51 as amended)](https://hrdf.org.sa/media/eacale2o/labor-low-en.pdf)

- Labour Law Art. 15 requires the employer to notify the labour office of establishment data including "the number of the Commercial Register or the license, its date and issuing authority" — the CR number is an establishment-registration datum, not a mandated payslip field — [Labour Law, BoE English print](https://saudi.it.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Download-Saudi-Labor-Law-PDF.pdf)
- Labour Law Art. 92 lists the only deductions permitted without the worker's written consent (employer loans up to 10% of wage; social insurance and other statutory contributions; thrift-fund contributions; housing-scheme instalments; disciplinary fines and damage deductions; judicially ordered debts up to one quarter of wage) and Art. 93 caps total deductions at half the due wage (up to three quarters only by labour-court decision) — [Labour Law, HRDF English consolidation](https://hrdf.org.sa/media/eacale2o/labor-low-en.pdf)
- Labour Law Art. 94: wrongful deductions or unjustified late payment can be taken to the labour court, which "may ... impose on the employer a fine not exceeding twice the amount deducted from the worker's wage or twice the outstanding wages" — [Labour Law, HRDF English consolidation](https://hrdf.org.sa/media/eacale2o/labor-low-en.pdf)
- Labour Law Art. 61(1): the employer must refrain from "withholding the worker's wage or part thereof without court order" — [Labour Law, HRDF English consolidation](https://hrdf.org.sa/media/eacale2o/labor-low-en.pdf)
- Contradicted vendor claim: a Qoyod guide says "Article 70" of the Labour Law ties employee rights to the salary receipt and requires a copy kept in the personnel file for employment plus five years — [Qoyod, salary receipt](https://academy.qoyod.com/en/templates/salary-receipt/); but the actual Art. 70 concerns disciplinary fines ("Nor may a worker be fined for a single violation an amount in excess of a five-day wage...") — [Labour Law, BoE English print](https://saudi.it.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Download-Saudi-Labor-Law-PDF.pdf)
- Contradicted secondary claim: a Mondaq-hosted article states that under "2025 amendments" employers "must issue monthly electronic payslips showing gross salary, deductions, working hours, and allowances, and keep these records for at least five years" without citing any article — [Mondaq, Saudi Labor Law Amendments: Key Changes in 2025](https://www.mondaq.com/saudiarabia/employee-rights-labour-relations/1627664/saudi-labor-law-amendments-key-changes-in-2025); no such provision appears in the HRDF consolidated Labour Law text or in the 2025 IR PDF cited above, and the King & Spalding summary of the 2024 amendments lists no payslip or wage-record change — [King & Spalding, Amendments to the Saudi Labor Law Approved](https://www.kslaw.com/news-and-insights/amendments-to-the-saudi-labor-law-approved)
- 2024 amendment metadata: Royal Decree M/44 dated 08/02/1446H, published in Umm Al-Qura 23 Aug 2024, in force 180 days later (19 Feb 2025); MHRSD described it as 38 articles amended, 7 deleted, 2 added — [Qanoonsa summary](https://qanoonsa.com/?p=4393); [Nezams summary](https://nezams.com/?p=5698); effective date also per [King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/news-and-insights/amendments-to-the-saudi-labor-law-approved)

### Inferences
- A payslip is best understood in Saudi practice as the per-worker extract of the IR Art. 5 wage register plus (where relevant) the Art. 73 fines register. A compliant payslip should therefore carry at minimum: worker identity (name, ID/Iqama no.), pay period and payment date (تاريخ الاستلام), basic wage and allowances (housing and other allowances, mirroring the WPS file fields in Q3), each deduction with its amount and stated reason (سبب الحسم), and net paid. Employer name, CR number and GOSI establishment number are not statutorily required on a payslip but are standard and help tie the slip to the Mudad wage file and Qiwa contract.
- Because deductions beyond Art. 92's list require the worker's written consent, a payslip that itemises deductions by legal category is the employer's best evidence of Art. 92/93 compliance.

### Gaps
- No official consolidated Arabic text of the Labour Law post-M/44 could be fetched (laws.boe.gov.sa returned HTTP 503 on three attempts); article numbering above was verified against the HRDF consolidation (which reflects the 2015 M/46 changes, e.g. Art. 90(2) mandatory bank payment and Art. 94 referencing "labor court") and the 2025 IR, which quotes Arts. 15, 17 and 20 unchanged.
- Could not retrieve the MHRSD "summary of amendments" table for M/44 to confirm positively that Arts. 17, 61 and 90 were untouched in 2024; the 2025 IR reproduces Art. 17 verbatim, which strongly suggests no change.

## Key Question 2: Does Saudi law accept electronic payslips? Is a worker signature/receipt required? What retention period applies to payroll records?

### Takeaway
Electronic form is expressly permitted for the wage register (IR Art. 5: "سواء أكانت ورقية أم الكترونية"), no provision requires a worker's signature on a wage receipt, and MHRSD itself frames the Wage Protection Program as the way to prove payment "without receipt sheets". No statute sets a payslip-specific retention period; the practical floor is the 12-month limitation period for labour claims (Art. 222) plus the time needed to defend late-filed or ongoing-employment claims, and the general PDPL rule that data may be kept only while needed for its purpose.

### Cited Findings
- IR Art. 5 applies to records kept "whether paper or electronic" (سواء أكانت ورقية أم الكترونية) — [Implementing Regulations 2025, MHRSD PDF](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2025-04/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%AD%D8%A9%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D9%86%D9%81%D9%8A%D8%B0%D9%8A%D8%A9%20%D9%84%D9%86%D8%B8%D8%A7%D9%85%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D9%85%D9%84%20%D9%88%D9%85%D9%84%D8%AD%D9%82%D8%A7%D8%AA%D9%87%D8%A7.pdf)
- MHRSD lists among WPS objectives for employers: "prove wage payment without receipt sheets (كشوفات الاستلام)", and states that "wage data in the program is the reference for resolving workers' complaints" — [MHRSD, Wage Protection Program page](https://hrsd.gov.sa/ar/initiatives/%D8%A8%D8%B1%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%AC-%D8%AD%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AC%D9%88%D8%B1)
- Labour Law Art. 90(2) (as amended 2015): "Firms shall be required to deposit workers' wages into their bank accounts through approved banks in the Kingdom, provided that their due date does not exceed the deadlines referred to above. The Minister may exempt some firms from such requirement." Art. 90(1): monthly-paid workers paid once a month; daily-paid at least weekly — [Labour Law, HRDF English consolidation](https://hrdf.org.sa/media/eacale2o/labor-low-en.pdf); the 2015 amendment history is confirmed by [GLMM, Royal Decree M/51 as amended by M/46](https://gulfmigration.grc.net/royal-decree-no-m51-issuing-the-saudi-labor-law-as-amended-by-royal-decree-no-m46-issued-on-05061436h/) (the original 2005 text read "Wages may be paid through accredited banks ... with the consent of the worker" — [BoE 2005 English print](https://saudi.it.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Download-Saudi-Labor-Law-PDF.pdf))
- The IR's model Work Organization Regulation (Annex 1, لائحة تنظيم العمل النموذجية) Art. 15 reads: "مع مراعاة أي إجراءات أو ترتيبات ينص عليها برنامج حماية الأجور؛ تدفع أجور العمال بالعملة الرسمية للبلاد في تاريخ .../.../... وتودع في حسابات العمال عن طريق البنوك المعتمدة في المملكة" (subject to any WPS procedures, wages are paid in the official currency on [date] and deposited in workers' accounts via approved banks) — [Implementing Regulations 2025, MHRSD PDF](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2025-04/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%AD%D8%A9%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D9%86%D9%81%D9%8A%D8%B0%D9%8A%D8%A9%20%D9%84%D9%86%D8%B8%D8%A7%D9%85%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D9%85%D9%84%20%D9%88%D9%85%D9%84%D8%AD%D9%82%D8%A7%D8%AA%D9%87%D8%A7.pdf)
- Evidence Law (نظام الإثبات): Art. 53 defines digital evidence as any evidence derived from data created, issued, delivered, stored or communicated by digital means and retrievable in understandable form; Art. 54 lists types including digital record, digital document, digital signature, "digital correspondence, including digital mail", communication means and digital media; Art. 57 makes unofficial digital evidence binding on the parties where (a) issued under the Electronic Transactions Law or E-Commerce Law, (b) derived from a digital means stipulated in the disputed contract, or (c) derived from a documented or publicly available digital means; Art. 59 gives it the authority of ordinary writing otherwise; Art. 61: a party refusing without excuse to produce what the court requests to verify digital evidence loses the right to rely on it or it is held against them; Art. 63: extracts from digital evidence have the same authority as the evidence to the extent they match — [Sahal Law Firm, means of proof under the Saudi Evidence Law](https://www.sahalfirm.com/t-en/blog/%D9%88%D8%B3%D8%A7%D8%A6%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D8%AB%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%B6%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A7-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AF%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%AC%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A9)
- Evidence Law Art. 30: signed correspondence, or correspondence whose attribution to the sender is established, has the authority of unofficial writing unless the sender proves they did not send it or authorise it — [Sahal Law Firm](https://www.sahalfirm.com/t-en/blog/%D9%88%D8%B3%D8%A7%D8%A6%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D8%AB%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%B6%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A7-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AF%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%AC%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A9)
- Latham & Watkins: Saudi law recognises electronic records as admissible evidence and treats them as originals where integrity is maintained; under the Evidence Law e-signatures are "digital evidence" and unofficial digital evidence is valid against transaction parties unless proven otherwise, with the burden on the challenger — [Latham & Watkins, Enforceability and Admissibility of Electronic Signatures in Saudi Arabia](https://www.lw.com/people/admin/upload/SiteAttachments/Understanding-the-Enforceability-and-Admissibility-of-Electronic-Signatures-in-Saudi-Arabias-Evolving-Legal-System.pdf)
- Since 12 May 2022 all new employment contracts must be electronically authenticated on Qiwa; authentication makes the contract "certified by the Ministry" and facilitates judicial verification of the relationship — [Clyde & Co, Electronic Employment Contracts through Qiwa](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2023/05/electronic-employment-contracts-through-qiwa)
- Labour Law Art. 222: no claim under the Law or a work contract is accepted "after twelve months following termination of the work relation", and no complaint of violations after twelve months from the violation — [Labour Law, HRDF English consolidation](https://hrdf.org.sa/media/eacale2o/labor-low-en.pdf); Clyde & Co confirms disputes must be filed at the labour office within 12 months of termination and that in practice employees file within 12 months of termination for violations during employment — [Clyde & Co, Part I: The labour dispute process in KSA](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2024/08/part-i-the-labour-dispute-process-in-ksa)
- Labour Law Art. 51: a contract is deemed to exist even if unwritten, and "the worker alone may establish the contract and his entitlements arising therefrom by all methods of proof" — [Labour Law, HRDF English consolidation](https://hrdf.org.sa/media/eacale2o/labor-low-en.pdf)
- Labour Law Art. 8: any waiver or settlement of the worker's statutory rights during the contract is void unless more beneficial to the worker — [Labour Law, HRDF English consolidation](https://hrdf.org.sa/media/eacale2o/labor-low-en.pdf) (relevant to any "acknowledgement" wording placed on a payslip)
- PDPL Art. 11(4): data no longer necessary for its purpose must be destroyed "without undue delay"; Art. 31 requires a record of processing activities including "the expected period for which Personal Data shall be retained" — [PDPL English text, SDAIA](https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/Documents/Personal%20Data%20English%20V2-23April2023-%20Reviewed-.pdf)
- GCC comparison: in the UAE's DIFC free zone, employees (other than short-term) are entitled to written payslips each pay period showing remuneration, deductions and their purpose, and payslips may be sent by email rather than hard copy; the onshore UAE Labour Law and Qatar/Oman regimes rely on WPS bank transfers rather than a general payslip mandate — [The National, Can a UAE employer refuse to provide a payslip (Jan 2025)](https://thenational-the-national-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/business/money/2025/01/27/can-a-uae-employer-refuse-to-provide-a-payslip); [PwC, GCC Wages Protection Systems](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/tax/documents/2019/gcc-wages-protection-system.pdf)

### Inferences
- An e-payslip delivered through a channel named in the employment contract or work regulation (e.g. "payslips will be issued via the company HR portal / to the worker's registered e-mail") falls squarely within Evidence Law Art. 57(b) ("digital means stipulated in the disputed contract"), making it binding on both parties; delivery via an ad hoc WhatsApp number does not benefit from that limb and would be assessed under Art. 59 / Art. 30 as ordinary writing or attributable correspondence.
- Proof of delivery an employer should keep: system logs (recipient, timestamp, document hash), bounce/read receipts where available, the worker's registered contact detail from the Art. 5 worker file, and, where feasible, an in-portal acknowledgement. A signature is not legally required; an "I accept the amounts" style acknowledgement adds little because Art. 8 voids waivers of statutory rights.
- Retention: with no statutory payslip period, keeping wage registers and payslips for the whole employment plus at least 12 months after termination (Art. 222) is the legal minimum; many employers adopt 5 years (a figure used in vendor literature and consistent with the 5-year accounting-record practice) or 10 years, but that is policy, not a Labour Law mandate. PDPL requires the chosen period to be documented in the ROPA.

### Gaps
- No MHRSD circular or guidance expressly addressing electronic payslips or acceptable delivery channels was found; the position above is derived from the IR's "paper or electronic" wording, WPS policy statements and the Evidence Law.
- No official statutory retention period for payroll records exists in the Labour Law/IR; vendor figures (e.g. five years) could not be traced to a legal text.

## Key Question 3: What do the Wage Protection System (Mudad) rules require, and do they substitute for payslips?

### Takeaway
WPS obliges every private-sector employer (final phase, 1-4 workers, mandatory since 1 Dec 2020) to pay wages in SAR through approved banks and to upload a monthly wage file (ملف حماية الأجور) via Mudad containing basic salary, housing allowance, other allowances, total deductions, net transferred, ID/Iqama, bank and transfer details; records are rejected if net does not equal basic + housing + other - deductions. MHRSD treats the WPS data as the reference for wage complaints and as proof of payment, but the WPS file is an employer-to-Ministry/bank submission, not a document given to the worker, so it does not legally or practically replace a payslip.

### Cited Findings
- Phased rollout by establishment size from 3,000+ workers (1 Sep 2013) to the final phase of 1-4 workers (1 Dec 2020); private schools and kindergartens were in phase 1 regardless of size — [MHRSD, Wage Protection Program page](https://hrsd.gov.sa/ar/initiatives/%D8%A8%D8%B1%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%AC-%D8%AD%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AC%D9%88%D8%B1)
- Wage file required fields: net transferred (الإجمالي), employee account number, name and bank, transfer description/number/status/date, basic salary (الراتب الأساسي), housing allowance (بدل السكن), other allowances, total deductions (حسم), National ID or Iqama number; accepted format is a standard TXT file — [MHRSD, WPS page](https://hrsd.gov.sa/ar/initiatives/%D8%A8%D8%B1%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%AC-%D8%AD%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AC%D9%88%D8%B1)
- Whole-file rejection: file opened after receipt from the bank; unified establishment number missing/mismatched; payment in a currency other than SAR. Record rejection: total not equal to basic + housing + other entitlements - deductions; negative values; zero total; missing ID/Iqama — [MHRSD, WPS page](https://hrsd.gov.sa/ar/initiatives/%D8%A8%D8%B1%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%AC-%D8%AD%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AC%D9%88%D8%B1); the same rejection rules are restated in MHRSD's WPS news item — [MHRSD news, WPS compliance](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/media-center/news/%D8%A8%D8%B1%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%AC-%D8%AD%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AC%D9%88%D8%B1-%D8%A8%D9%86%D8%B3%D8%A8%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%AA%D8%AB%D8%A7%D9%84)
- GOSI linkage: a violation is recorded if deductions exceed 50% of the basic salary registered with GOSI, if pay exceeds the GOSI-registered salary by 20%, or if basic salary does not match the GOSI-registered salary; compliance rate = workers paid through the program / total workers registered with GOSI — [MHRSD, WPS page](https://hrsd.gov.sa/ar/initiatives/%D8%A8%D8%B1%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%AC-%D8%AD%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AC%D9%88%D8%B1)
- Consequences stated by MHRSD: fine of SAR 10,000 per month for failing to pay wages on the due date; 2 months' upload delay = all services suspended except work-permit issuance/renewal; 3 months = all services suspended and workers may transfer to another employer without consent; inspection visit if file is a month late, payment late or amounts differ; referral to judicial authorities if non-payment is proven — [MHRSD, WPS page](https://hrsd.gov.sa/ar/initiatives/%D8%A8%D8%B1%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%AC-%D8%AD%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AC%D9%88%D8%B1)
- August 2025 Mudad rules: alerts for basic salary that is illogical relative to the worker's data, deductions above 50% of pay, basic wage not entered for over 90 days, and workers with no record of receiving pay; reminder when due, second notice after 10 days, final warning on day 15, inspection request if no file within 20 days; employer has 10 days to justify delay; employee has 3 days to accept/reject the justification — [Okaz, 13 Aug 2025](https://www.okaz.com.sa/economy/na/2208645); [DLA Piper, New wage monitoring system (22 Aug 2025)](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2025/new-wage-monitoring-system-to-crack-down-on-salary-violations)
- Scale and compliance: over 1 million establishments registered on Mudad (about 94% of private-sector establishments), over 10 million workers' wages documented, over 17 million wage files processed by end-2025, compliance above 85% in 2025 — [MHRSD news, 11 Mar 2026](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/en/media-center/news/%D8%A8%D8%B1%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%AC-%D8%AD%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AC%D9%88%D8%B1); 2024 compliance was reported above 88% across 900,000+ establishments — [MHRSD news (Arabic)](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/media-center/news/%D8%A8%D8%B1%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%AC-%D8%AD%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AC%D9%88%D8%B1-%D8%A8%D9%86%D8%B3%D8%A8%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%AA%D8%AB%D8%A7%D9%84)
- Draft penalties table (MHRSD, Sept 2025, not confirmed as final): late or withheld wages without legal justification up to SAR 3,000 per worker; failure to submit wage protection files monthly up to SAR 2,000; failure to document contracts electronically SAR 1,000 per worker — [Okaz, 15 Sep 2025](https://www.okaz.com.sa/news/local/2213367). Note the inconsistency with the SAR 10,000/month figure on the MHRSD WPS page and with vendor claims of "SAR 3,000 per affected employee" — [greytHR WPS guide 2026 (vendor, unsourced)](https://www.greythr.com/middle-east/blog/saudi-arabia-wps-mudad-compliance-guide-2026/)
- Labour Law Art. 229 (as amended): general penalties up to SAR 100,000, closure up to 30 days or permanent closure, doubled on repetition, and "fines shall be multiplied by the number of persons subject of the violation"; Art. 230 empowers the Minister to issue the violations-and-penalties table — [Labour Law, HRDF English consolidation](https://hrdf.org.sa/media/eacale2o/labor-low-en.pdf)
- The WPS page does not mention payslips or giving workers wage statements; it says only that the program lets employers prove payment without receipt sheets and that program data is the reference for complaints — [MHRSD, WPS page](https://hrsd.gov.sa/ar/initiatives/%D8%A8%D8%B1%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%AC-%D8%AD%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AC%D9%88%D8%B1)
- Domestic workers were brought into WPS via Musaned in phases from July 2024 with full coverage from 1 Jan 2026 — [greytHR WPS guide 2026 (vendor)](https://www.greythr.com/middle-east/blog/saudi-arabia-wps-mudad-compliance-guide-2026/)
- GOSI deductions that a payslip should show: for Saudis hired before 3 July 2024, employee 9.75% (9% pension + 0.75% SANED) and employer 11.75% (9% + 2% occupational hazards + 0.75% SANED); new entrants from 3 July 2024 see the pension share rise 0.5 points each July to 11% each; expatriates have no employee deduction, employer pays 2% occupational hazards — [ZenHR, GOSI deductions guide 2026](https://blog.zenhr.com/en/how-to-calculate-gosi-deductions-in-saudi-arabia-complete-guide-2026); [Teamed, Saudi GOSI and 2025 amendments](https://www.teamed.global/gulf-employment-law/saudi-gosi-and-2025-amendments)

### Inferences
- The WPS wage file fields (basic, housing, other allowances, deductions, net, ID, bank, date) are the de facto minimum data model for a Saudi payslip; aligning payslip line items to the WPS file and to the Qiwa contract wage removes reconciliation disputes and supports the "basic salary matches GOSI" check.
- Because Mudad already validates basic salary against GOSI and flags deductions above 50%, a payslip that shows GOSI-insurable wage and the GOSI deduction separately helps workers and inspectors reconcile the three systems (Qiwa contract, Mudad file, GOSI wage).
- The WPS record proves that a net amount reached the bank; it does not prove to the worker how that net was computed. The payslip therefore remains the only worker-facing breakdown of deductions, which matters under Art. 92-94.

### Gaps
- The ministerial decision number and date that originally created WPS (2013) were not found on MHRSD pages; MHRSD cites only "labour regulations" and inspection rules. The Mudad site (mudad.com.sa) returned HTTP 503.
- The official penalties figure for late payment is inconsistent across MHRSD's own page (SAR 10,000/month) and the Sept 2025 draft table (SAR 3,000/worker); the final adopted table could not be retrieved.

## Key Question 4: PDPL implications of sending payslips over WhatsApp or personal/unencrypted email (lawful basis, security, cross-border transfer, data subject rights, breach notification, penalties)

### Takeaway
Payslip contents are personal data under PDPL Art. 1(4) (which expressly lists bank numbers and records). The lawful basis for issuing payslips is contract/legal obligation under Art. 6(2) (no consent needed), but the choice of channel is a security decision under Art. 19 and IR controls; sending via WhatsApp or Gmail is a "transfer or disclosure to a party outside the Kingdom" under Art. 29 (servers abroad) that must fit a permitted purpose and either an adequacy finding (none published as of Sept 2026), an appropriate safeguard (SCCs/BCRs/certification), or the contract-necessity derogation, plus risk assessment where safeguards are relied on. SDAIA's October 2026 draft IR amendments would add a data-localisation requirement (store in KSA), making foreign-hosted consumer channels riskier. No SDAIA guidance specific to messaging apps was found.

### Cited Findings
- PDPL Art. 1(4): personal data is "Any data, regardless of its source or form, that may lead to identifying an individual ... including name, personal identification number, addresses, contact numbers, license numbers, records, personal assets, bank and credit card numbers, photos and videos"; Art. 1(8) "Disclosure: Enabling any person - other than the Controller or the Processor ... to access, collect or use personal data by any means"; Art. 1(9) "Transfer: The transfer of Personal Data from one place to another for Processing" — [PDPL English text, SDAIA](https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/Documents/Personal%20Data%20English%20V2-23April2023-%20Reviewed-.pdf)
- PDPL Art. 5(1): no processing without consent "except for the cases stated in this Law"; Art. 6: consent not required where "(2) ... the Processing is pursuant to another law or in implementation of a previous agreement to which the Data Subject is a party" or "(4) ... necessary for the purpose of legitimate interest of the Controller, without prejudice to the rights and interests of the Data Subject, and provided that no Sensitive Data is to be processed" — [PDPL English text, SDAIA](https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/Documents/Personal%20Data%20English%20V2-23April2023-%20Reviewed-.pdf); legitimate interest was added by the 2023 amendments and is unavailable for sensitive data — [CMS, Saudi Arabia's updated PDPL](https://cms.law/en/omn/legal-updates/saudi-arabia-s-updated-personal-data-protection-law)
- Art. 11(2): collection methods must be "direct, clear and secure"; Art. 11(3): content limited to the minimum necessary; Art. 12: a privacy policy must be made available before collection specifying purposes, data, means of collection, processing, storage and destruction, and rights — [PDPL English text, SDAIA](https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/Documents/Personal%20Data%20English%20V2-23April2023-%20Reviewed-.pdf)
- Art. 19: "The Controller shall implement all the necessary organizational, administrative and technical measures to protect Personal Data, including during the Transfer of Personal Data, in accordance with the provisions and controls set out in the Regulations" — [PDPL English text, SDAIA](https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/Documents/Personal%20Data%20English%20V2-23April2023-%20Reviewed-.pdf); the Implementing Regulations require security measures aligned with controls issued by the National Cybersecurity Authority (NCA) or recognised best practices where NCA controls do not apply, so non-compliance with applicable NCA controls could itself expose a controller to SDAIA action — [Latham & Watkins, Saudi Arabia's data protection law enters into force (Dec 2023)](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/2023/12/Saudi-Arabias-data-protection-law-enters-into-force)
- Art. 8: controllers may only select processors "providing the necessary guarantees" and must monitor their compliance — [PDPL English text, SDAIA](https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/Documents/Personal%20Data%20English%20V2-23April2023-%20Reviewed-.pdf)
- Art. 20: controller must notify SDAIA of "any breach, damage, or illegal access to personal data" per the Regulations, and notify the data subject where it "would cause damage to their data or cause prejudice to their rights and interests" — [PDPL English text, SDAIA](https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/Documents/Personal%20Data%20English%20V2-23April2023-%20Reviewed-.pdf); IR/SDAIA guidance: notify SDAIA within 72 hours of awareness via the National Data Governance Platform with a description of the breach, when/how it occurred, categories and number of affected data subjects and potential consequences; notify data subjects "without undue delay" through their usual channel (SMS or email) with no size threshold — [Baker McKenzie, Saudi Arabia publishes guidance on data breach notification (Oct 2024)](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/saudi-arabia-publishes-guidance-on-data-breach-notification/); L&W notes there is "no specific risk threshold" in the 72-hour rule — [Latham & Watkins (Dec 2023)](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/2023/12/Saudi-Arabias-data-protection-law-enters-into-force)
- Art. 29(1): transfer or disclosure outside the Kingdom is permitted for (A) an agreement to which the Kingdom is party, (B) the Kingdom's interests, "(C) ... the performance of an obligation to which the Data Subject is a party", or (D) other purposes in the Regulations; Art. 29(2): must not prejudice national security, must go to a jurisdiction with protection at least equivalent to the PDPL per SDAIA assessment, and be limited to the minimum data; Art. 29(4): Regulations set exemptions from (2)(B) and (C) — [PDPL English text, SDAIA](https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/Documents/Personal%20Data%20English%20V2-23April2023-%20Reviewed-.pdf)
- Transfer Regulations (amended 1 Sept 2024): Art. 2 adds purposes (central processing operations, providing a service/benefit to the data subject, scientific research); Art. 4 appropriate safeguards are SCCs, Binding Common Rules and a Certificate of Accreditation, which exempt the controller from the adequacy condition (and, per Baker McKenzie's reading, from the separate minimum-data condition); risk assessment required before any safeguard-based transfer and for continuous/large-scale sensitive-data transfers; SDAIA's adequacy list had not been published as of September 2026; SDAIA-licensed certification bodies operate under rules issued 17 Feb 2026 — [Securiti, Regulation on Personal Data Transfer Outside the Kingdom](https://securiti.ai/regulation-on-personal-data-transfer-outside-the-kingdom); [Baker McKenzie via Global Compliance News, Sept 2024](https://www.globalcompliancenews.com/2024/09/17/https-insightplus-bakermckenzie-com-bm-data-technology-saudi-arabia-updates-data-transfer-regulations-and-introduces-the-first-set-of-standard-contractual-clauses_09042024/)
- SCCs published 1 Sept 2024 in four versions (C2P, C2C, P2C, P2P); modifying them renders them invalid; importers must submit to KSA law — [Baker McKenzie via Global Compliance News](https://www.globalcompliancenews.com/2024/09/17/https-insightplus-bakermckenzie-com-bm-data-technology-saudi-arabia-updates-data-transfer-regulations-and-introduces-the-first-set-of-standard-contractual-clauses_09042024/)
- Derogations: absent adequacy or a safeguard, transfers may still occur where "necessary to perform a contract with the relevant data subject", to protect vital interests, with consent, or for legal claims — [Latham & Watkins (Dec 2023)](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/2023/12/Saudi-Arabias-data-protection-law-enters-into-force)
- Draft IR amendments under consultation 6 Oct - 5 Nov 2026 would add to IR Art. 23 a requirement that controllers store personal data within the Kingdom (transfers abroad still permitted under the PDPL), keep the 72-hour SDAIA notice in Art. 24 without a harm threshold, expand DPIAs (Art. 25), make registration in the National Register of Controllers mandatory for entities making cross-border transfers (Art. 34), and require privacy notices in the language customarily used with data subjects (likely Arabic); if adopted they enter into force 60 days after gazette publication — [Clyde & Co, SDAIA publishes draft amendments to the Implementing Regulations (Oct 2026)](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/10/sdaia-publishes-draft-amendments-to-the-pdpl)
- Penalties: Art. 35: disclosing/publishing sensitive data in violation of the Law with intent to harm or for personal benefit, imprisonment up to two years and/or fine up to SAR 3 million (prosecuted by the Public Prosecution); Art. 36: for other violations a warning or fine up to SAR 5 million, doubled for repeat violation, imposed by a SDAIA committee of at least three members including a technical specialist and legal adviser, appealable to court; Art. 40: any individual harmed may claim proportionate compensation for material or moral damage — [PDPL English text, SDAIA](https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/Documents/Personal%20Data%20English%20V2-23April2023-%20Reviewed-.pdf)
- Enforcement status: PDPL in force 14 Sept 2023 with compliance expected by 14 Sept 2024; SDAIA "actively enforcing" as of Feb 2026 — [DLA Piper, Data Protection Laws of the World: Saudi Arabia](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=SA); 48 enforcement decisions announced as of mid-January 2026, with violation categories reported as processing/disclosing personal data without legal justification, failing to implement organisational and technical safeguards, and marketing without consent; controllers get only five days to respond to a SDAIA notification, 60 days to appeal — [Clyde & Co, Enforcement of the Saudi PDPL is live (Mar 2026)](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/03/enforcement-of-the-saudi-pdp-law); [Securiti PDPL overview](https://securiti.ai/?p=107027)
- DPO required where the controller is a public entity processing at scale, where core activities involve regular, continuous large-scale monitoring, or where core activities involve sensitive data; controllers must also maintain a ROPA and register on the National Data Governance Platform in specified cases — [DLA Piper, Saudi Arabia](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=SA); [Latham & Watkins (Dec 2023)](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/2023/12/Saudi-Arabias-data-protection-law-enters-into-force)
- Employer-specific framing: multinationals handling Saudi staff data must comply; payroll files are in scope; payroll processing abroad may need new arrangements due to transfer limits; employees should receive a privacy notice covering purpose and legal basis; one HR-software vendor argues remote access from abroad to Saudi personal data is an export under Art. 29 — [SHRM, Saudi Arabia data protection law](https://rss.shrm.org/link/22131/15157074/saudi-arabia-data-protection-law.aspx); [IceHrm, Data sovereignty in HR software (vendor view)](https://icehrm.com/blog/data-sovereignty-in-hr-software-a-2026-buyers-guide/)

### Inferences
- Lawful basis: issuing payslips and processing salary data to do so rests on Art. 6(2) (performance of the employment contract and compliance with Labour Law Art. 17 / WPS). Consent is neither needed nor advisable as the basis because Art. 5(2) lets the worker withdraw it at any time. Consent could, however, be used narrowly for the worker's choice of a personal channel (e.g. opting into a personal e-mail or messaging number) provided the employer still meets Art. 19 security duties, since consent does not cure an insecure method.
- Channel analysis under Art. 29 and the Transfer Regulations: WhatsApp (Meta, US/EU infrastructure) and Gmail/Outlook.com personal mailboxes involve disclosure to a party outside KSA. The strongest route is the Art. 29(1)(C) purpose (obligation to which the data subject is party) combined with the contract-necessity derogation, but "necessity" is weak when a KSA-hosted portal or corporate e-mail is available; SCCs cannot be signed with WhatsApp/Gmail as consumer services. A KSA-hosted employee self-service portal or corporate e-mail (ideally with in-country tenancy and encryption at rest and in transit) avoids the Art. 29 problem entirely and aligns with the proposed Art. 23 localisation rule.
- Security under Art. 19/IR (NCA-aligned controls): unencrypted e-mail attachments and consumer chat apps do not meet "secure" collection/transfer in Art. 11(2)/19 terms where more secure means are reasonably available; a mis-sent payslip (wrong number, group chat) is a reportable breach within 72 hours under Art. 20/IR Art. 24, with no de minimis threshold, and triggers worker notification where prejudice to rights is possible. Password-protected PDFs with out-of-band passwords, per-recipient addressing checks and expiring links materially reduce (but do not remove) the Art. 29 issue.
- Data subject rights: workers may access and correct payslip data and request destruction when no longer needed; the ROPA must state the payroll retention period (Art. 31).

### Gaps
- No SDAIA guideline, FAQ or enforcement decision specifically about messaging apps (WhatsApp) or personal e-mail for employee data was found in English or Arabic searches; the Turkish, Singaporean and UK regulators' positions surfaced instead and are not Saudi authority.
- The Implementing Regulations' article numbers for security (Art. 23), breach (Art. 24), DPIA (Art. 25) and registration (Art. 34) are taken from Clyde & Co's description of the 2026 draft; the SDAIA English IR PDF itself could not be fetched (WAF block), so wording is secondary-sourced.
- No published SDAIA adequacy list exists (as of Sept 2026), so no destination country can currently be assumed adequate.
- None of the 48 reported SDAIA enforcement decisions is publicly described as an employer/payroll case.

### Inferences
- A NEOM contractor-compliance officer should expect any NEOM audit to test at least the Saudi statutory baseline (Art. 90 bank payment, WPS compliance rate, IR Art. 5 wage register, Art. 92/93 deduction limits, no passport retention) plus ILO-aligned expectations (ILO Convention 95 style wage transparency: workers informed of wage conditions and given particulars of each payment). Issuing itemised payslips in a language the worker understands is the practical way to evidence "ILO policies" compliance even though no public NEOM text mandates it.

## Key Question 6: MHRSD guidance or labour-court precedent on disputes where payslips were missing; 2024-2026 enforcement actions, fines or inspection campaigns on payslips/wage transparency

### Takeaway
No Saudi labour-court judgment or MHRSD guidance that turns specifically on a missing payslip was found. The operative Saudi rules are structural: MHRSD designates WPS data as "the reference for resolving workers' complaints", the worker may prove entitlements "by all methods of proof" (Art. 51), and labour courts can fine employers up to twice any wrongfully deducted or delayed wages (Art. 94). Enforcement in 2024-2026 has focused on Mudad-driven wage monitoring (Aug 2025 alert rules, 90-day/50% thresholds), a proposed penalties table (Sept 2025) and compliance-rate publication, not on payslip issuance per se.

### Cited Findings
- MHRSD: "wage data in the program is the reference for resolving workers' complaints" and the program exists partly to "provide a reference for wage disputes" — [MHRSD, WPS page](https://hrsd.gov.sa/ar/initiatives/%D8%A8%D8%B1%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%AC-%D8%AD%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AC%D9%88%D8%B1)
- Labour Law Art. 51: "the worker alone may establish the contract and his entitlements arising therefrom by all methods of proof"; Art. 95: where neither contract nor work regulation fixes the wage, the wage for similar work in the firm, then professional custom, then the labour court's estimate "in accordance with the dictates of justice" applies — [Labour Law, HRDF English consolidation](https://hrdf.org.sa/media/eacale2o/labor-low-en.pdf)
- Dispute process: claims are filed electronically with MHRSD's Amicable Settlement Department (two sessions within 21 working days), then referred to the Labour Court; employees are exempt from court fees; parties must file written statements with supporting evidence; appeals within 30 days via Najiz; claims up to SAR 50,000 are final — [Clyde & Co, Part I: The labour dispute process in KSA (Aug 2024)](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2024/08/part-i-the-labour-dispute-process-in-ksa)
- Unpaid-wage complaints in practice are evidenced by payslips for the last 6-12 months, bank transfer records and the Qiwa contract; HRSD can trace WPS records to verify whether a payment was processed — [Global Law Experts, Non-payment of wages and final settlement delays in Saudi Arabia (Aug 2026, practitioner note)](https://globallawexperts.com/nonpayment-wages-final-settlement-delays-saudi/)
- Evidence Law Art. 61: a party who refuses, without acceptable excuse, to produce what the court requests to verify digital evidence loses the right to rely on it or has it held against them — [Sahal Law Firm](https://www.sahalfirm.com/t-en/blog/%D9%88%D8%B3%D8%A7%D8%A6%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D8%AB%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%B6%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A7-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AF%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%AC%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A9)
- GCC analogue (not Saudi law): Abu Dhabi Labour Court (reported 24 June 2025) held that wages are the worker's right which the employer can discharge only by written evidence, acknowledgment or oath (UAE Civil Transactions Law Art. 912(1)), placing the burden of proving payment or non-entitlement on the employer, and awarded AED 110,400 — [Okaz, 24 Jun 2025 (UAE case)](https://www.okaz.com.sa/variety/na/2201699); [Slaati, Jun 2025](https://slaati.com/2025/06/24/p2707210.html); Egypt's Court of Cassation (appeal 2820/94) likewise held that failure to prove payment obliges the employer to pay — [Parlmany, Oct 2026](https://www.parlmany.com/News/2/616882/)

### Inferences
- In a Saudi wage dispute the employer's practical evidentiary burden is to show what was paid and why any deduction was made; the Mudad file proves the net transfer, but only the IR Art. 5 wage register / payslip shows the deduction and its reason as Art. 92-94 require. An employer unable to produce either risks the Art. 94 double-amount fine and an adverse inference under Evidence Law Art. 61 if the court requests the digital records.
- Delivery-channel choice is unlikely to be litigated as such; what matters is that the employer can retrieve, per worker and per month, an authentic record (hash/log) of the slip and its contents.

### Gaps
- No Saudi labour-court or Court of Appeal judgment addressing payslips, nor any published Supreme Court principle on burden of proof for wage payment, was located; Saudi judgments are not systematically published and Arabic searches returned UAE/Egyptian authority instead.
- The adopted (final) MHRSD penalties table for 2025/2026 and the exact fine for failing to keep the IR Art. 5 registers were not retrieved; figures above come from press reports of a draft and an "updated" table.
- No MHRSD inspection campaign specifically on payslips or wage transparency (as distinct from WPS/Mudad compliance) was found for 2024-2026.
